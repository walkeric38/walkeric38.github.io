Neural Amp Modeling Guitar Amplifier

Our team is building a portable, battery-powered guitar amplifier with Neural Amp Modeler (NAM) running on board. It brings modeled tube-amp tones, touchscreen effects controls, and a built-in speaker into one self-contained amp.

Players will be able to select amplifier models and dial in effects such as delay and reverb. We’re targeting 6–8 hours of playing time per charge and an open-source design that hobbyists can build, modify, and make their own.

My contributions

  • System architecture and component selection
  • Touchscreen interface development using C, LVGL, and ESP32-S3
  • Hardware bring-up, integration, and testing

Audio hardware

The team’s custom carrier board connects the Daisy Seed3 audio processor with the amplifier’s inputs and controls.

Custom Arduino Uno

The objective of this project was to design a custom Arduino Uno that preserves the functionality of the commercial board while reducing switching noise.

The layout incorporates a continuous bottom ground plane to provide a low-inductance return path and help minimize ground bounce and switching noise. A custom switching-noise shield enables direct comparison with a commercial Arduino Uno to measure and characterize the effect of the PCB layout on noise performance.

PCB Design · Board 2

Comparing Switching Noise with Good and Bad Layout

I designed, assembled, and tested two electrically identical hex-inverter circuits with different PCB layouts, driven by a common 555 timer operating at approximately 500 Hz.

The good layout uses a continuous ground plane and a decoupling capacitor placed close to the inverter’s power pin. The bad layout uses ground traces in place of a continuous plane and positions the decoupling capacitor farther from the IC.

Test points provide access to the quiet-HIGH outputs, quiet-LOW outputs, and board-level power rail. Comparing switching-noise measurements at these points allows the effects of grounding and decoupling layout to be evaluated.

PCB Design · Board 1

Astable Multivibrator

I designed, assembled, and tested a 555 timer circuit targeting approximately 500 Hz and a 60% duty cycle. Four LEDs use 10 kΩ, 1 kΩ, 300 Ω, and 50 Ω series resistors to demonstrate how load resistance affects brightness.

After breadboard prototyping, I created the schematic and PCB layout in Altium Designer. The board includes a ground plane, power indicator, isolation switches, labeled components, and accessible test points for measuring circuit voltages and LED current.
